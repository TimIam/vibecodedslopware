Desert Chase

A roadrunner forages in the creosote. A coyote watches from the brush, works up its nerve, and goes for it. It fails. It always fails. Then it gets up, shakes off the dust, and starts planning the next one.

How it works

  • Real animals, not cartoons. Both were modeled and rigged from reference photos, then exported into the page. The coyote stalks low, the roadrunner pecks at the dirt and cocks its tail when something feels off.
  • Every wipeout is physics. When it goes wrong the coyote turns into a ragdoll, so no two tumbles are the same. It lies there a moment, gets up, shakes, and slinks off to think about it.
  • Six ways to lose. The bird cuts away at the last second and the coyote cartwheels or skids out. A pounce lands face-first on bare ground. The bird leads it into a cactus, over a rock, or across a dry wash it does not see. Or the bird simply leaves it behind.
  • The desert is new every time. Creosote, saguaro, cholla, prickly pear, rocks, a wash and far ridges, all generated and lit for the hour you pick. It is shot on a long lens, so the far brush and the near sand fall softly out of focus, like a nature documentary.

Things you can do

  • Toss a beetle: click the ground and the roadrunner comes over to eat it, wherever the coyote happens to be.
  • Spook the bird: click near it and it sprints away from the spot.
  • Send the coyote: start an attempt right now instead of waiting for it to get brave.
  • Customize: the light (early morning, midday, golden hour, dusk), the lens (close, medium, wide), how often the coyote tries (now and then, often, relentlessly), and slow motion on the wipeouts.

Every monitor

  • In Chrome or Edge, open Customize and click Every monitor. The browser asks to manage windows on your displays; say yes, then click once per extra screen and once inside each new window. One desert stretches across all of them, and the chase runs from screen to screen.
  • The first visit spends a few seconds baking the desert before the animals show up. After that it holds a smooth frame rate.
  • Add #embed to the address for just the desert with no controls, handy for kiosk and web-screensaver setups.
